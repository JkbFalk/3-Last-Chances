1. Short vs Detailed
Short Descriptions should tell the player what the button does conceptually and strategically. Remove scaling formulas, decimal points, and minor conditions.
Detailed Descriptions should tell the player exactly how the game calculates the outcome. This is for the theorycrafters.


2. Rule for Numbers:
Short: Only show final outcomes and durations. (e.g., "Deals 200[D]" "[Stuns] for 2 sec.")
Detailed: Show the final outcome followed by the (Math) in parentheses. (e.g., "Deals 200[D] ({0}%[WD]).")

3. Rule for Icons & Naming:
Short: Use the Icon (e.g., [I], [S], [H], [A]).
Detailed: Use the Icon + Full Name (e.g., [I]Injury, [S]Stagger, [H]Health, [A]Armor).

4. Standardization of Verbs

Deal: Used only for direct damage (Injury, Stagger, Magic Damage).
Correct: "Deal 50[I]." / Incorrect: "Inflict 50[I]."
Apply: Used only for stacking effects or debuffs.
Correct: "Apply 50[Burn]." / Incorrect: "Deal 50[Burn]."
Grant / Gain: Used for positive effects on the player or allies.
Correct: "Gain 20[A]." / Incorrect: "Receive 20[A]."
Restore: Used for recovering resources that have caps (Health, Energy, Stagger Bar, Ammo).
Correct: "Restore 20[H]." / Incorrect: "Heal 20[H]."
Inflict: Used only for Hard/Soft Crowd Control.
Correct: "Inflict [Stun] for 2 sec." / Incorrect: "Apply [Stun] for 2 sec."

6. Syntax Formatting & Color Coding

Resources & Stats: Always prefixed by their icon and colored appropriately.
<color=#E20101>[I]Injury</color>, <color=#5700FF>[S]Stagger</color>, <color=#4350FF>[E]Energy</color>
Status Effects (Buffs/Debuffs): Always bolded and prefixed by their icon.
[Burn]Burn, [Sharp]Sharp, [Onslaught]Onslaught.
Crowd Control / Mechanical States: Always bracketed and bolded. No icons unless specifically needed.
[Stunned], [Flinching], [Stealth], [Invincible].
Actions / Triggers: Capitalized to show they interact with game mechanics.
"When you Dodge...", "Upon a successful Riposte...", "Your Basic Attacks..."

7. Hierarchy of Information

A detailed tooltip should always follow the same structural flow so players know where to look for specific information.
Header Info: Tags, Costs, Cooldowns.
The Trigger/Action: How is the ability used?
The Primary Effect: What happens immediately?
The Secondary Effect / Conditionals: What happens if conditions are met?
Template:
[Ability Tags] (Cost).
[The Action], dealing [Primary Effect].
If [Condition], then [Secondary Effect].
Example: Ability_WindRush_Ultimate
Following the Hierarchy:
[Charge-Time Technique] ({0} sec).
Dash toward your target, dealing {0}[I]Injury ({1}% [HI]) and {2}[S]Stagger ({3}% [HS]).
Creates a wind barrier that pushes away secondary enemies. The primary target is trapped in the center and takes {4}[S]Stagger ({5}% [HS]) per second. Lasts {6} seconds.

8. Upgrade/Ultimate Formatting
When writing descriptions for Upgrades (Mastery A, Mastery B, Ultimate), do not repeat what the base ability does. Only state what changes or what is added.
Rule of Thumb: Treat Upgrades as "Patch Notes" for the ability.
Use bullet points or distinct paragraph breaks for separate mechanical additions.
Example: Ability_Fireball_MasteryA
Standard:
Increases explosion radius by 50%.
Now inflicts [Stun] ({0} - {1} sec, based on distance from center).
